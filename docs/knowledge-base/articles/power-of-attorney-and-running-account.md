# Power of Attorney and Running Account Authorization With a Broker

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain what these two authorisations are, why they are optional, and how an investor can protect themselves, without saying whether any user should sign one.

## Core Concepts

- A **power of attorney (PoA)** is a document of high importance because it can give someone else rights and access over the investor's accounts and money. In the securities market a client may execute a PoA in favour of the stock broker and/or depository participant so they can operate the demat and bank account for delivery of shares and pay-in and pay-out of funds (BSE booklet).
- Under SEBI guidelines, **signing a PoA is not mandatory**: it is purely optional and voluntary, and it can be **revoked at any time**. A specific PoA, as per SEBI's guidelines, is what may be executed.
- If a broker transfers shares from the investor's account for any reason other than those the investor authorised, the booklet says to take it up with the broker immediately, and if there is no response or the answer is unsatisfactory, with the depository or exchange.
- A **running account authorization** lets the broker keep an investor's funds or shares in a separate account with the broker, adjusting them against future settlement obligations. Normally settlement has to be done within 24 hours of pay-out, but for investors who trade often, the authorization eases transactions.
- It is **voluntary, dated and revocable at any time**. The broker must settle the actual funds and securities at least once in a stated period (30 or 90 days as the client chooses, per the booklet).
- The booklet advises: read and understand all documents before signing, **do not sign any blank document**, check the do's and don'ts in the account opening kit, and register your own mobile number and email with the bank, depository participant and broker to receive alerts.
- The rules on both authorisations have been revised since the booklet's date; verify the latest rules on SEBI's website or with the depository.
- Keeping an eye on alerts, contract notes and statements is how an unauthorised movement is noticed (see the contract-note article).
- A PoA is different from a nomination: a PoA is about operating the account during the holder's life; a nomination is about who may receive the holdings after death.

## Worked Example

A hypothetical investor opens a trading and demat account and is handed a form with a box for a PoA and another for running account authorization.

1. They read what each form allows, whether it is specific (limited to settlement purposes) and how to revoke it.
2. They decide, on their own, to sign neither, one, or both; none is required to open the account under SEBI's guidelines as described in the booklet.
3. If they sign, they note the date, keep a copy, and check each contract note and alert.
4. Later, shares leave the account in a transfer they did not authorise. They write to the broker at once and keep the reply; if unresolved they approach the depository and exchange.

The scenario is invented to show the sequence; it does not recommend signing or refusing.

## Misconceptions

- "I must sign a PoA to trade." The booklet describes it as optional and voluntary.
- "Once signed it is permanent." It can be revoked at any time.
- "A running account means my money is locked." It is an authorization to adjust balances, with periodic settlement and the right to revoke.
- "It is safe to sign a form with blanks to be filled later." The booklet advises never to sign a blank document.

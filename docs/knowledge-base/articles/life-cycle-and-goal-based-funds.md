# Life Cycle, Retirement and Children's Funds: Goal-Based Fund Designs

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain what a goal-based or life cycle fund is, how a glide path works in principle, which lock-ins and rules apply to retirement and children's funds, and that the exact rules and mixes must be checked in current documents.

## Core Concepts

- AMFI's booklet describes **solution-oriented schemes**: a **retirement fund** has a lock-in of at least five years or until retirement age, whichever is earlier; a **children's fund** has a lock-in of at least five years or until the child reaches the age of majority, whichever is earlier.
- It also describes **life cycle funds**: goal-based funds available across different maturity periods whose investments **change over time as they move closer to maturity**.
- The idea is a **glide path**: more in growth assets (equity) when the goal is far away, and gradually more in safer assets as the goal date approaches, so that a fall just before the money is needed hurts less.
- The **National Pension System (NPS)** has the same idea in its **Auto Choice** option, described in SEBI's Beginner's Guide as a life cycle fund: contributions are allocated automatically among the three asset classes (equity, corporate debt and government securities) in a predefined manner depending on age. The guide also describes an **Active Choice** in which the subscriber chooses the mix within limits. The details in that guide are older; check PFRDA for the current options and limits.
- A glide path is **rule-based**: it moves along a schedule whether markets are high or low, and it does not predict markets.
- **Trade-offs:** a lock-in reduces the chance of dipping into the money early but also reduces liquidity; a glide path reduces risk near the goal but can lower expected growth. It does not guarantee a particular outcome or protect against loss.
- A product with the label "retirement" or "children's" is **not guaranteed to meet the goal**; the outcome depends on contributions, time, costs and market returns.
- Fees and what the fund actually holds should be read in the scheme documents, as with any fund, and a **riskometer** level applies to each scheme.
- The rules, lock-in periods and mixes above are described in older booklets; verify the current ones in the scheme documents and with PFRDA before relying on them.
- Whether a goal-based fund suits a person depends on their own goal, horizon and capacity for loss, which the article does not judge.

## Worked Example

A hypothetical glide path, with invented mixes and no real scheme in mind:

| Years to goal | Growth assets (equity) | Safer assets (debt and government securities) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | 80% | 20% |
| 15 | 65% | 35% |
| 5 | 35% | 65% |
| 1 | 15% | 85% |

Suppose the portfolio is ₹10,00,000 a year before the goal, and equity falls 30% in that final year.

- At the 15% equity mix: the equity part of ₹1,50,000 loses ₹45,000, so the portfolio falls by 4.5%.
- Had the mix still been 80% equity: ₹8,00,000 would lose ₹2,40,000, a fall of 24%.

The older guide's NPS table also shows the equity share falling as age rises, from half of contributions for the youngest members to a small share in the mid-fifties; those exact figures are dated and should be checked with PFRDA before use.

## Misconceptions

- "A life cycle fund guarantees enough for the goal." It follows a rule for its mix; returns are not guaranteed.
- "Locked in means safe." A lock-in limits withdrawals; the value can still fall.
- "All goal-based funds use the same glide path." Each scheme sets its own, in its documents.
- "I can ignore a glide-path fund once I buy it." Costs, contributions and the goal itself still need review.

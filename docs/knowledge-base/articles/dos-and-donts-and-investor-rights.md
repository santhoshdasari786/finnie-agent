# Do's and Don'ts of Investing in Securities, and an Investor's Rights and Obligations

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to list the booklet's practical do's and don'ts, explain what an investor can expect from a broker and what the investor must do in return, and say that the booklet is dated and current rules should be confirmed.

## Core Concepts

**Do's (from Annexure I of the booklet):**
- Consult a SEBI-registered investment adviser if advice is needed; invest in a scheme or product that matches your own objective and risk appetite.
- Insist on a valid contract note or confirmation memo for trades within 24 hours, and keep track of your portfolio in your demat account regularly.
- Read all documents carefully before signing, note all charges, fees and brokerage applicable to your accounts, and keep records of signed documents, statements, contract notes and payments.
- Review your financial needs and goals, and the portfolio, from time to time.
- Pay for transactions through banking channels only, not in cash.
- Keep your information updated with the broker and depository participant (address, bank details, email, mobile number). Because SIM cards can be ported, keep one mobile number attached to your accounts.
- Use the nomination facility for all your investments; get a running account settled periodically; check daily SMS and email from the exchange and the monthly report of funds and securities balances.

**Don'ts:**
- Don't borrow money for investment.
- Don't deal with unregistered brokers or other unregistered intermediaries.
- Don't pay more than the agreed brokerage or charges.
- Don't sign any blank form or delivery instruction slip; don't execute any document without understanding its terms; don't issue a general power of attorney, and if one is needed, make it very specific.
- Don't engage in **dabba trading**, which is illegal and offers none of the safety of trades on a recognised exchange.
- Don't rely on hot tips: a person offloading securities that may not be marketable may be behind them, and spreading hot tips is illegal and should be reported to SEBI.
- Never share your online password; change passwords often.
- Don't fall for Ponzi schemes, unregistered chit funds, unregistered collective investment or deposit schemes.
- In a dispute, file a written complaint with the intermediary, exchange or SEBI within a reasonable time.

**Rights of an investor (Annexure II):** get a unique client code from the broker; get copies of the KYC and other documents executed; have trades executed only in your own code; get the best price; get a contract note; ask for details of charges levied; receive funds and securities on time; receive account statements as agreed and ask for settlement of accounts.

**Obligations:** complete KYC and provide supporting documents; understand the voluntary conditions being agreed with the trading member; place orders in line with the norms agreed with the member.

These lists are from a booklet issued around 2020, so confirm the current rules with SEBI, the exchange or the broker.

## Worked Example

A hypothetical investor uses the lists as a monthly checklist after trading.

1. Compare each contract note with their own record of orders (rights: contract note, details of charges).
2. Check that the daily and monthly alerts match their expectations.
3. Confirm that no blank or unexplained form is outstanding with the broker, and that mobile and email details are current.
4. Notice a charge higher than the agreed brokerage, ask for details in writing, and escalate to the exchange or SEBI if unresolved.

The steps illustrate the habits; they are not a guarantee of how any dispute ends.

## Misconceptions

- "Dabba trading saves costs." The booklet says it is illegal and has none of the protection of exchange trades.
- "A hot tip from a friend is safe because it is free." The booklet warns that tips can come from someone trying to offload securities.
- "Giving a broker a general power of attorney is routine." The booklet advises against a general one and says to issue a very specific one only if needed.
- "Rights only apply if I trade a lot." The rights listed apply to any investor dealing through a broker.

# Fixed Deposits and Recurring Deposits

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain how fixed and recurring deposits work, what features vary from bank to bank, how deposit insurance relates to them, and how to think about their returns after inflation and tax, without naming a bank or an interest rate as best.

## Core Concepts

- A **fixed deposit (FD)** "involves placing funds with the bank for a fixed term at a certain interest rate" (SEBI Financial Education Booklet). **Tenure and rate of interest on FDs vary from bank to bank**, and senior citizens may get extra benefits on the rate.
- A **recurring deposit (RD)** is a fixed amount deposited at monthly intervals for a predetermined term. It "earns higher interest than savings bank account" (same booklet), and tenure and rate also vary by bank.
- **Interest is taxable**, and banks deduct tax at source (TDS) once interest crosses a stipulated amount (SEBI booklet). The exact threshold and rates change; see "Pre-Tax vs. Post-Tax Returns".
- **Early withdrawal.** An FD is a contract for a term. Withdrawing before maturity usually brings a lower interest rate or a penalty set by the bank; check the bank's terms before depositing.
- **Loans against deposits.** The SEBI booklet notes it is "possible to get loans from banks against fixed deposits up to 75 to 90% of the deposit amount".
- **Tax-saver FD.** A special term deposit with a 5-year lock-in, no premature withdrawal and no loan against it, historically eligible for a deduction under the old tax regime (SEBI booklet). Eligibility depends on the current law.
- **Safety.** Bank deposits are regulated by the RBI and insured by DICGC up to a limit per depositor per bank (see "Deposit Insurance and Its Limits"). Bank deposits are preferred "more for their liquidity and safety than for the returns" (SEBI Beginner's Guide).
- **Company fixed deposits** are different: "similar to bank fixed deposits but entail lesser liquidity and usually carry higher risk and return" (SEBI Beginner's Guide). They are not covered by bank deposit insurance.
- **Inflation risk.** If the interest rate is lower than inflation, the deposit's purchasing power falls even though the number of rupees grows (see "Nominal vs. Real Returns").

## Worked Example

A hypothetical ₹1,00,000 FD at an assumed 7% a year, with interest added to the deposit each year (annual compounding). The rate is illustrative, not a current offer:

| Years | Maturity value |
|---|---|
| 1 | ₹1,07,000.00 |
| 3 | ₹1,22,504.30 |
| 5 | ₹1,40,255.17 |

Banks often compound interest more often than yearly, such as quarterly, which changes the maturity value slightly. If inflation averages 5% over the same period, the *real* growth is far smaller than the nominal growth shown. If interest is taxed each year, the after-tax growth is smaller still.

## Misconceptions

- "All fixed deposits are insured without limit." Bank deposits are insured only up to the DICGC limit per depositor per bank; company deposits are not covered.
- "FD interest rates are the same everywhere." They vary by bank, tenure and depositor category.
- "FD interest is tax-free or taxed only on maturity." Interest is generally taxable as income each year it accrues, and TDS may apply.
- "A guaranteed rate means a guaranteed real return." The rate is fixed in rupees; its value in purchasing power depends on inflation.

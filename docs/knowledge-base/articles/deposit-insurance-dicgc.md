# Deposit Insurance and Its Limits in India

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain what deposit insurance is, the current per-depositor limit as published by DICGC, how accounts are aggregated, and what is not covered, and to point users to DICGC's site for the current rules.

## Core Concepts

- The **Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC)** insures bank deposits. It insures "all deposits such as savings, fixed, current, recurring, etc." in insured banks (DICGC FAQs).
- **Which banks:** all commercial banks, including branches of foreign banks, local area banks and regional rural banks, and all co-operative banks, are insured. **Primary co-operative societies are not insured by DICGC.** The scheme is compulsory: no insured bank can withdraw from it.
- **The limit:** "Each depositor in a bank is insured up to a maximum of ₹5,00,000 (Rupees Five Lakhs) for both principal and interest amount held by him in the same right and same capacity", measured on the date the bank's licence is cancelled, a merger or reconstruction scheme takes effect, or the RBI imposes "all inclusive directions" restricting withdrawals.
- **Principal and interest together:** DICGC's example: an account with principal ₹4,95,000 plus accrued interest ₹4,000 is insured for ₹4,99,000. If principal were ₹5,00,000, the interest would not be insured, "not because it was interest but because that was the amount over the insurance limit".
- **Aggregation within one bank:** deposits in different branches of the same bank are added together. All accounts held in the same name, in the same capacity, in one bank (savings, current, fixed, recurring) are combined and share one limit.
- **Different banks are separate:** "If you have deposits with more than one bank, deposit insurance coverage limit is applied separately to the deposits in each bank."
- **Different capacities are separate:** accounts held as an individual, as a partner of a firm, as a guardian of a minor, as a director of a company, as a trustee, or jointly may each have their own limit. For joint accounts, names must be in the same order to count as the same capacity.
- **What is not covered:** deposits of foreign governments, deposits of Central and State Governments, inter-bank deposits, amounts due on deposits received outside India, and amounts the Corporation has specifically exempted with RBI approval.
- **Set-off:** a bank can deduct dues owed by the depositor, and the insurance applies after netting.
- **Who pays and how fast:** the premium is paid entirely by the insured bank. DICGC pays the liquidator the claim within two months of receiving the claim list; it does not deal directly with depositors.
- **What is not deposit insurance:** mutual funds, shares, bonds, company fixed deposits and insurance policies are not covered by DICGC.

## Worked Example

Illustration of aggregation using the limit stated by DICGC (₹5,00,000). The scenarios are hypothetical:

| Depositor holds | Insured at that bank |
|---|---|
| Savings ₹2,00,000 + FD ₹4,00,000 in the same bank, in her own name | ₹5,00,000 in total (the two are added; the excess ₹1,00,000 is not insured) |
| ₹5,00,000 at Bank A and ₹5,00,000 at Bank B, in her own name | Up to ₹5,00,000 at each bank, separately |
| ₹5,00,000 in her own name and ₹5,00,000 in a joint account with her husband at the same bank | Each may be a different capacity, so each can have its own limit |

The figures illustrate how the rules work; the limit itself is published by DICGC and can be changed.

## Misconceptions

- "All my money in a bank is safe up to any amount." Insurance has a limit per depositor per bank and capacity.
- "Splitting money across many accounts at the same bank multiplies cover." Same-capacity accounts in one bank are added together.
- "Mutual funds and company FDs are insured too." They are not covered by DICGC.
- "DICGC pays me directly." In a liquidation, payment goes through the liquidator.

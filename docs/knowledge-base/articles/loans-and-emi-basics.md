# Loans and EMIs: Types of Loans, Collateral and How an EMI Works

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain what a loan and an EMI are, name the common loan types, explain collateral and the lender's assessment, and show how the total interest on a loan is calculated, without suggesting whether to borrow.

## Core Concepts

- **Borrowing** is taking money and paying it back over a period. SEBI's booklet says it is used when savings are inadequate for a goal, and gives the flexibility of repaying in small instalments. **Equated Monthly Instalments (EMIs)** are a popular way to repay.
- **Collateral** is a property or other asset offered to the lender as security. If the borrower stops repaying, the lender can seize the financed asset and the collateral to cover its loss. The lender's claim over the collateral is called a **lien**.
- **Types of loans** in the booklet: **personal** (any purpose, no collateral), **vehicle**, **education** (the student normally needs an admission offer first), **gold** (against gold as security), **housing** (construction, renovation, extension, buying property or land), **agriculture** and **consumer durable** loans.
- **Documents** commonly asked for include proof of identity and residence, recent bank statements and salary slips, photographs and proofs of any collateral.
- **The 5 Cs of credit** that banks consider, per the booklet: **capacity** (ability to repay), **character** (trustworthiness), **capital** (the borrower's own money at stake), **collateral**, and **conditions** (the purpose of the loan).
- The **interest rate**, tenure and fees determine the cost; rates are set by lenders and change with the market and the borrower's profile.
- An EMI is a fixed monthly payment. **Early in the loan most of each EMI is interest**, and the share going to principal rises over time as the outstanding balance falls.
- **Total cost** = EMI × number of months, minus the amount borrowed, gives the interest paid. A longer tenure lowers the EMI but raises the total interest.
- A loan taken to invest adds leverage to the investment (see the leverage article); an investment can fall while the loan stays.

## Worked Example

A hypothetical loan of ₹10,00,000 at an assumed 9% a year, repaid monthly. The rate is invented, and fees and taxes are ignored. EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1) with r = 0.09 ÷ 12 per month.

| Tenure | EMI | Total repaid | Total interest |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 years (120 months) | ₹12,668 | ₹15,20,109 | ₹5,20,109 |
| 20 years (240 months) | ₹8,997 | ₹21,59,342 | ₹11,59,342 |

In the first month of the 10-year loan, the interest is ₹10,00,000 × 0.0075 = ₹7,500, so ₹7,500 of the ₹12,668 EMI is interest and only ₹5,168 reduces the principal.

Halving the EMI burden by doubling the tenure more than doubles the interest paid. The figures were computed in code and are for illustration only.

## Misconceptions

- "A lower EMI means a cheaper loan." A longer tenure lowers the EMI but increases total interest.
- "Each EMI repays the same share of principal." Early EMIs are mostly interest.
- "Collateral is only a formality." The lender can seize it if repayments stop.
- "Any loan is fine if I can afford the EMI." Affordability also depends on income stability, other commitments and the total cost.

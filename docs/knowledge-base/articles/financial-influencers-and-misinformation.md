# Financial Influencers and Investment Misinformation

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain why tips from social media personalities ("finfluencers") need checking, what registration and disclosure mean, what conflicts of interest to look for, and how to verify a claim, without naming or judging any individual.

## Core Concepts

- A **finfluencer** is a person who talks about investing on social media or video platforms. Many are not registered with SEBI, so they are not authorised to give investment advice or recommendations (SEBI board paper, July 2024).
- SEBI's July 2024 board paper proposed that persons regulated by SEBI, and their agents, should not associate with unregistered persons who give advice or recommendations. The paper is a proposal; the final rules should be read from SEBI before relying on them.
- SEBI's annual report chapter on investor protection (published August 2026) says SEBI has mandated that all regulated entities and their agents prominently disclose their registered name and SEBI registration number on social media, to help investors tell regulated content from that of unregistered persons.
- The same chapter describes SEBI's "SEBI vs SCAM" campaign, whose videos caution against unregistered investment advisers, finfluencers, pump-and-dump schemes, deepfake scams, courses promising guaranteed returns, and taking loans to trade, with the message to research thoroughly before acting on tips.
- **Conflicts of interest** to look for: payment or free products for promoting something, commissions for sign-ups, affiliate links, and an interest in the price of the thing being promoted. A sponsored view is not necessarily wrong, but it is not neutral.
- **Selective evidence:** screenshots of profits, a few winning calls, or returns without the losses and the period are not evidence of a track record (see survivorship bias).
- **Pressure and urgency** ("last chance", "limited slots", "guaranteed") are warning signs, as are requests to move to a private chat group or pay for a course to get the "real" tips.
- **A practical check:** confirm whether the person or firm appears on SEBI's registered-intermediary records, use SEBI's verification tools, and compare the claim with an official source such as the fund's offer documents.

## Worked Example

A hypothetical video says: "This small company will double in three months. Link in the description."

1. Who is speaking, and is a SEBI registration number shown? If not, the speaker is not offering regulated advice.
2. Is there a disclosure of payment, a holding or a link that earns a commission?
3. Is any evidence given beyond past wins, and do the claims include risks and losses?
4. Does the promise include a guaranteed or very high return in a short time? That alone is a reason to stop and check an official source.

The example is invented. The questions help judge the claim; they do not say whether the stock is a good or bad investment.

## Misconceptions

- "Lots of followers means the person is qualified." Popularity is not registration or a track record.
- "If the advice is free, there is no conflict." The money can come from sponsorship, commissions or the promoter's own holdings.
- "A screenshot of profits proves skill." It shows one outcome, selected after the fact.
- "Registered means guaranteed." Registration shows regulation, not that the advice will make money.

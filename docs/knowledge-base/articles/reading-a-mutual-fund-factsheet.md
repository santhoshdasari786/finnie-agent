# Reading a Mutual Fund Factsheet

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to walk a user through the typical sections of a mutual fund factsheet, explain what each section tells them, and flag what a factsheet alone can't tell them.

## Core Concepts

A typical factsheet includes:

- **Investment objective** — the fund's stated goal and strategy (e.g. "long-term capital appreciation by investing primarily in large-cap equities"). The first thing to check: does the objective match what the investor actually wants?
- **Benchmark** — the index the fund measures itself against. A fund should be compared to its *own* stated benchmark, not an unrelated index, to judge relative performance fairly.
- **Portfolio holdings** — the actual securities currently held, often with sector/asset-type breakdowns. Useful for checking whether the fund's real holdings match its stated objective (a fund labeled "large-cap" holding significant mid/small-cap exposure, for instance, is worth noticing).
- **Expense ratio** — see "Expense Ratios and the Impact of Fees" — the recurring cost, disclosed here.
- **Risk-o-meter / risk disclosure** — a standardized risk-level indicator and accompanying disclosures required by SEBI regulation, intended to communicate the fund's risk category at a glance. The framework itself has been revised more than once since its introduction (e.g. the number of levels and its visual format have both changed via subsequent SEBI circulars) — don't assume the format described in an older article/blog is the current one; AMCs must recalculate and can disclose a scheme's risk level monthly, so it can change even without a regulatory update.
- **Fund manager and track record** — who manages it and since when; useful context, though past performance of a manager is not a guarantee of future results (see "Why Past Performance Doesn't Guarantee Future Returns").
- **Historical returns** — typically shown across multiple periods (e.g. 1-year, 3-year, 5-year, since-inception), usually alongside the benchmark's returns for the same periods for comparison.

## Worked Example (What to Check, Not Real Fund Data)

A simple review checklist when reading any factsheet:

1. Does the stated objective match what I'm looking for?
2. Is the benchmark comparison showing the fund ahead, behind, or in line with its benchmark, across *multiple* time periods (not just the most recent one)?
3. Does the expense ratio look reasonable relative to similar funds with the same objective?
4. Does the risk-o-meter/risk category match my own risk tolerance?
5. Do the actual portfolio holdings look consistent with the stated objective/category?

## Misconceptions

- A factsheet shows historical performance and current holdings — it does not predict future returns.
- Comparing a fund's returns to the *wrong* benchmark (e.g. comparing a debt fund to an equity index) produces a meaningless comparison — always check what the fund's *own* stated benchmark is.
- A single period's outperformance (e.g. "best returns last year") is a weak signal on its own — check consistency across multiple periods, not just the most recent one.

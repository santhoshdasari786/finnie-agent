# Contract Notes, Alerts and Account Statements for Share Investors

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain what documents and alerts an investor receives after trading, how to use them to check an account, and where to take a discrepancy, while noting that timelines and rules in this area are updated.

## Core Concepts

- A **contract note** is the evidence of a trade done by the broker. The BSE booklet calls it a legal document with details of the transaction: the securities bought or sold, the traded price, the time of the trade, brokerage and so on.
- It can be issued on paper or electronically. An electronic contract note needs a specific authorisation to the broker with the investor's email ID, and is digitally signed and encrypted. The booklet says it should be preserved for future reference, especially if a dispute arises.
- The booklet states that the broker issues the contract note within 24 hours of the trade, and advises the investor to cross-check it against their own record of the order and to raise any discrepancy with the broker immediately.
- **Record of client order:** brokers are required to keep evidence of the client placing an order, which can be a signed physical record, a telephone recording, an email from an authorised address, an internet transaction log, mobile message records or another legally verifiable record.
- **SMS and email alerts:** investors can opt in, free of cost, to alerts for trading and demat activity. For this, mobile numbers and email IDs must be shared and kept updated with the broker and depository participant.
- **Trade verification:** exchange websites have a trade verification module to check the trades executed in an account, with data available the next day (T+1, as stated in the booklet).
- **Statements:** investors are entitled to periodic reports from brokers and depositories, free of cost, and a statement of funds and securities balances as on a stated annual date from the broker. Anything not understood can be clarified with the bank, depository, depository participant, exchange or broker, or via SEBI's helpline.
- **Consolidated Account Statement (CAS):** a single statement showing a month's transactions across all mutual funds and other securities in the demat accounts. PAN must be updated with the broker or depository participant to receive it.
- **Funds and securities for settlement:** the booklet cautions that not having enough funds or securities by the settlement pay-in date may lead to penalties and losses.
- These details come from a booklet published around 2020. Settlement cycles, timelines and processes have been revised since then; confirm current rules with SEBI, the exchange or the broker.

## Worked Example

A hypothetical investor places an order for 50 shares through an app and later receives a contract note.

1. They compare the contract note with the order: security, quantity (50), price, time, and the brokerage and charges listed.
2. The quantity is 50 and the security is right, but the brokerage is higher than the broker's published schedule.
3. They raise the difference with the broker in writing and keep the note. If there is no satisfactory answer, they use the exchange or SEBI's complaint channels (see the grievance article).
4. At month end they check that the CAS shows the same holding.

The steps illustrate checking, not any particular charge.

## Misconceptions

- "The app screen is enough proof." The contract note is the formal record of the trade; the booklet advises keeping it.
- "Alerts are optional extras." They are the investor's early warning of activity they did not do, provided contact details are current.
- "A statement I do not understand can be ignored." The booklet advises clarifying it with the relevant entity.
- "The booklet's timelines are current." They are from an older edition; check today's rules.

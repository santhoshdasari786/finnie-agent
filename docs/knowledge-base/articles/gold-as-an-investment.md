# Gold as an Investment

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain the common forms of holding gold, what each form involves in cost and regulation, and what gold does and does not do for a portfolio, without suggesting how much gold anyone should hold.

## Critical Constraint

Gold products, their taxation and their regulation change. Which Sovereign Gold Bond series are open, how gold ETFs and gold receipts are taxed, and which platforms are regulated should be **confirmed from current SEBI, RBI and Income Tax sources**. This article explains the structures, not current terms.

## Core Concepts

- **Gold is a real asset** that produces no interest or dividend. Any return comes from a change in its price. SEBI's Financial Education Booklet lists gold among the non-financial products people invest in, along with land and silver.
- **Price drivers** (general knowledge): global gold prices, the rupee's exchange rate against the dollar, interest rates and general demand. These can move prices up or down for long periods.
- **Physical gold** (jewellery, coins, bars) involves making charges, purity checks, storage and security, and a difference between buying and selling prices. Jewellery is bought for use as well as investment.
- **Gold ETFs** are mutual fund schemes that invest primarily in gold or gold-related instruments and trade on exchanges (SEBI circular on Gold ETFs, via search). Gold funds are also available as mutual fund schemes (SEBI Beginner's Guide lists gold funds). They are regulated by SEBI, carry an expense ratio, and need a demat account for ETFs.
- **Electronic Gold Receipts (EGRs)** are issued by a vault manager against gold deposited with it and are notified as securities traded on an exchange (SEBI's FAQs on the Gold Exchange, via search).
- **Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs)** have been issued by the RBI on behalf of the Government of India and are among the products available via the RBI Retail Direct platform (per RBI's FAQ via search). Whether a new series is open must be checked.
- **"Digital gold" sold by private platforms** is a different thing from the SEBI-regulated forms above. Whether a given product is regulated by SEBI, and what protections apply, should be confirmed before relying on it. A SEBI advisory on this was not located in this pass.
- **Role in a portfolio:** gold is often discussed as a diversifier because its price does not always move with shares, but that relationship can change (see "Diversification and Correlation").

## Worked Example

A hypothetical comparison of three ways to hold ₹1,00,000 worth of gold, described without prices because charges and rules vary:

| Form | Visible costs | Storage | Regulatory frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| Physical coin or bar | Premium over market price, resale gap | Own responsibility | None specific to the investor |
| Gold ETF | Expense ratio, brokerage, bid-ask spread | In demat account | SEBI-regulated mutual fund scheme |
| Electronic gold receipt | Exchange and vaulting charges | Vault manager holds gold; receipt in demat | SEBI-notified security |

All three move with the gold price. They differ in costs, convenience and the protections around them.

## Misconceptions

- "Gold is risk-free." Gold prices can fall, sometimes for years, and gold earns no income.
- "All digital gold is the same as a gold ETF." They are different structures with different regulation.
- "Gold always protects against inflation or falling shares." It has done so in some periods and not in others.
- "Buying jewellery is an efficient way to invest in gold." Making charges and resale deductions can be significant.

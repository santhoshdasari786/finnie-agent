# Bank Accounts and Digital Payment Modes in India

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to describe the common account types and compare NEFT, RTGS, IMPS and UPI, saying that charges, limits and timings are set by banks and RBI and can change.

## Core Concepts

- SEBI's booklet lists reasons to keep money in a bank rather than cash at home: cash can be stolen or lost, it earns no interest, and bank deposits help create eligibility for loans. Commercial banks in India are regulated by the Reserve Bank of India and are inspected and audited at intervals.
- **Savings bank account:** low interest but highly liquid; supports ATM access; interest is not subject to tax deducted at source on the balance but is taxable in the depositor's hands (as the booklet describes); can be single or joint; a minor can open one through a guardian.
- **Basic savings bank deposit account (BSBDA):** introduced for financial inclusion, a zero-balance account with relaxed limits on deposits and withdrawals and a free ATM card and passbook.
- **Fixed deposit (FD):** funds placed for a fixed term at a stated interest rate; tenure and rate vary by bank, and interest may attract tax deducted at source beyond a stipulated amount. **Recurring deposit (RD):** a fixed amount each month for a fixed term, earning more than a savings account. (See the FD and RD article.)
- **Special tax-saving term deposit:** a deposit with a five-year lock-in, no premature withdrawal or loan against it.
- **Deposit insurance** covers deposits up to a limit per depositor per bank under the government scheme; see the DICGC article for the limit and the conditions.
- **NEFT** (National Electronic Funds Transfer): bank-to-bank transfer using the beneficiary's account number and IFSC code; there is no minimum or maximum amount per the booklet; credit takes some hours.
- **RTGS** (Real Time Gross Settlement): real-time transfer between banks, used for high-value transactions, within a specified period on working days.
- **IMPS** (Immediate Payment Service): instant transfers between bank accounts.
- **UPI** (Unified Payments Interface): instant transfer from a smartphone using a virtual payment address, available around the clock and through a UPI-enabled bank app.
- **Charges and limits** differ by bank and mode and have changed over time; check the bank and RBI for current values.
- The booklet also describes RBI's newer categories, **payments banks** (which cannot give loans or issue credit cards) and **small finance banks**.

## Worked Example

A hypothetical person needs to do three things.

1. **Pay a friend a small amount at midnight:** UPI or IMPS fit, as the booklet describes them as instant, with UPI available at all hours.
2. **Transfer a large amount to a seller's account for a property deposit:** RTGS is described as the mode for high-value, real-time transfers; they check the bank's current minimum amount and timings first.
3. **Save a fixed amount each month for a known purchase in two years:** a recurring deposit fits the pattern described, with the rate and tenure confirmed with the bank.

The choices show how the features map to needs, not a recommendation.

## Misconceptions

- "A savings account is the best place for all money." It is liquid but pays low interest, so inflation can erode it.
- "UPI and NEFT are the same." They differ in timing, availability and how the payee is identified.
- "FD interest is tax-free." The booklet notes tax deducted at source beyond a stipulated amount, and interest is taxable.
- "Deposit insurance covers any amount." It covers a limited amount per depositor per bank.

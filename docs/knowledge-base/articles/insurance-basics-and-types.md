# Insurance Basics: What It Is and the Main Types

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain what insurance does, name the main life and general policy types with their features, and explain how a combined insurance and investment policy differs from pure cover, without recommending any policy.

## Core Concepts

- **Insurance** lets individuals and businesses protect themselves against significant potential losses at a reasonably affordable cost. It is a form of **risk management** for an uncertain future loss (SEBI booklet).
- It is a promise of compensation for specific potential future losses in exchange for a periodic payment called a **premium**. The company providing it is the **insurer**; the buyer is the **insured** or policy holder; the contract is the **policy**, which sets out the conditions under which compensation is paid.
- **Term insurance:** if the insured dies during the policy term, the nominees receive the **sum assured** chosen at purchase. It is active for a fixed period, the "term".
- **Endowment insurance:** pays a lump sum after a specific term (on maturity) or on the death of the insured. Typical maturities mentioned are ten, fifteen or twenty years, up to a certain age; some pay on critical illness too.
- **Whole life:** stays active as long as premiums are paid.
- **Unit-linked insurance (ULIP):** a combination of insurance and an investment vehicle. A portion of the premium pays for cover and the remainder is invested in equity and debt instruments.
- **Other types** in the booklet: personal accident cover (compensation for bodily injury, disability or death caused solely by an accident), motor insurance, **health insurance** (medical and surgical expenses), travel insurance, property insurance and group insurance (covering a defined group, such as employees).
- **Insurance is for protection against loss; investing is for growth.** Policies that combine both, such as endowment and ULIPs, have to be compared on both parts: the cover and the charges against it, and what is invested and at what cost.
- **IRDAI** (Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India) regulates the insurance sector to protect policyholders and ensure systematic growth; grievances about insurance go to the channels in the grievance chapter.
- The amount of cover needed, the suitable type and the premium depend on a person's circumstances, which the article does not assess.

## Worked Example

A hypothetical household compares two things.

- A **term policy** with a sum assured of ₹1,00,00,000 for 25 years and a yearly premium of ₹12,000 (invented). If the insured dies during the term, nominees receive the sum assured. If the insured outlives the term, nothing is paid, since term insurance is pure protection.
- An **endowment policy** with a higher premium pays a lump sum at maturity or on death, so part of the premium goes to cover and part to a savings-like component, whose return should be compared fairly (see the comparing products article).

The comparison shows the structure: what is paid, when, and what each premium buys. It does not say which is right for anyone, and the figures are invented.

## Misconceptions

- "Insurance is an investment." Pure insurance pays on a specified event; investment-linked policies combine the two and should be judged on both parts.
- "If I never claim, the premiums are wasted." The premium buys protection for the period, whether or not a loss occurs.
- "All life insurance pays out at the end." Term insurance pays only if the insured dies in the term; endowment pays on maturity or death.
- "Insurance is regulated by SEBI." Insurance is under IRDAI; SEBI regulates the securities market (ULIPs have both an insurance and an investment side).

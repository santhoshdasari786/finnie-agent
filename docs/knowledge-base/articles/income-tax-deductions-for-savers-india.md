# Income Tax Deductions for Savers in India: Sections 80C, 80CCD(1B), 80D and Others

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain what a tax deduction is, name the sections SEBI's booklet lists for savers and what each covers, and tell the user where to check current limits and rules, without quoting limits from memory or recommending where to invest.

## Core Concepts

- A **deduction** is an amount the law lets a taxpayer subtract from income before tax is calculated. It lowers taxable income; the tax saved depends on the taxpayer's slab, so the same deduction saves different amounts for different people.
- SEBI's booklet explains that income tax is governed by the Income Tax Act, 1961 as amended by later Finance Acts, and that the tax year runs from 1 April to 31 March. Income earned in the **previous year** is taxed in the **assessment year**.
- **Section 80C** covers money put into life insurance, provident fund, ELSS mutual fund schemes, five-year special bank deposits, Tier 1 NPS contributions, National Savings Certificates, and repayment of housing loan principal, among others (booklet list).
- **Section 80CCD(1B)** is an extra deduction for NPS investment over and above the 80C amount.
- **Section 80D** covers medical insurance for self, spouse, dependent parents and dependent children.
- **Section 80G** covers donations to listed funds and institutions.
- **Section 80TTA** covers tax charged on savings bank interest, subject to limits and conditions, for individuals and HUFs.
- **Section 24** covers interest paid on a housing loan taken to buy, build or repair property.
- A deduction does not make an investment good. A product that lowers tax can still have a lock-in, costs or poor returns; the pre-tax versus post-tax article explains how to compare fairly.
- The booklet itself warns that dates and details are subject to change and readers should check the latest provisions. Limits, which sections apply and whether a deduction is available under the newer tax regime are exactly the things that change, so none are quoted here.

## Worked Example

A hypothetical taxpayer has taxable income of ₹10,00,000 before any deduction and puts ₹1,00,000 into an eligible 80C product.

- Taxable income becomes ₹9,00,000.
- If the taxpayer's top slab were 20%, the tax saved on that ₹1,00,000 would be ₹20,000, before any cess.
- The slab, the amount and whether the deduction is allowed at all are all invented for illustration. Under rules that do not allow the deduction, the saving would be zero.

The point is the mechanism: the saving is the deduction multiplied by the marginal slab, not the deduction itself.

## Misconceptions

- "A deduction is a refund of the money invested." It reduces taxable income; the saving is only a part of the amount.
- "Tax-saving products are automatically good investments." Lock-ins, costs and risk still matter.
- "Everyone can claim every section." Eligibility depends on the taxpayer type, the rules in force and the tax regime chosen.
- "Limits never change." They are set in law and revised in Finance Acts; verify the current figure with the Income Tax Department or a qualified tax professional.

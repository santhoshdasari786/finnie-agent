# Market Capitalization and Company Size (Large, Mid and Small Cap)

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain what market capitalization is, how Indian mutual funds label companies as large, mid or small cap, and why the label says something about size but not about quality or safety.

## Core Concepts

- **Market capitalization** ("market cap") is the value of a company as the market prices it: the current price of one share multiplied by the total number of shares outstanding (Investor.gov glossary).
- It changes every day as the share price moves, and it is not the same as what the company owns, earns or would fetch if sold.
- **Large, mid and small cap** are size labels. For equity mutual fund schemes in India, SEBI's circular of 6 October 2017 defines them by rank, using full market capitalization: large cap is the 1st to 100th company, mid cap the 101st to 250th, and small cap the 251st company onwards.
- Because the bands are ranks, not rupee amounts, the rupee value that separates the groups moves with the market.
- Mutual funds must use the list of stocks prepared by **AMFI**. Where a stock is listed on more than one exchange, the average of its full market capitalization on those exchanges is used. The list is updated every six months, using data as at the end of June and December.
- A company can move from one group to another between lists, and a fund labelled "mid cap" may hold stocks whose group has changed since it bought them.
- A size label describes size only. Smaller companies are often more volatile and harder to trade in quantity, and large companies can still fall sharply, but a label alone does not give a risk level; the scheme's riskometer and documents do.
- **Free-float** market capitalization (only shares available to the public) is a different measure from the "full" market capitalization SEBI uses for these bands; index providers often use free-float.

## Worked Example

A hypothetical company has 20 crore shares outstanding and its share price is ₹500.

- Market capitalization = ₹500 × 20 crore = ₹10,000 crore.
- If the price rises to ₹550 the next month with the same share count, market capitalization becomes ₹11,000 crore, a rise of 10%.
- Whether this makes it a large, mid or small cap depends on its rank among all listed companies on the current AMFI list, not on the ₹10,000 crore figure by itself. The agent should not guess the rank.

The numbers are invented to show the arithmetic; they do not describe any real company.

## Misconceptions

- "A high share price means a big company." Market cap is price times number of shares. A company with a high price and few shares can be smaller than one with a low price and many shares.
- "Large cap means safe." Large companies can lose value, and the label does not remove market risk.
- "Small cap means a poor business." It means a lower rank by market value; many small companies are sound, and the label says nothing about quality.
- "The cut-off is a fixed rupee amount." SEBI's definition is by rank, and AMFI refreshes the list every six months.

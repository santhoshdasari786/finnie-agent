# Credit Cards and Debit Cards: How They Differ and Using Them Safely

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain the difference between a credit and a debit card, say what happens if a credit card balance is not paid in time, and list the safety practices from SEBI's booklet, without recommending any card.

## Core Concepts

- A **credit card** gives the holder the benefit of a **credit facility** for a period: payments can be made without having the money in hand at that moment (SEBI booklet). The money is lent by the issuing bank.
- A **debit card** is issued to a bank account holder to withdraw cash at ATMs and pay at shops and online. The money comes **from the holder's own savings or current account** at the same bank.
- Both can be used to withdraw cash at ATMs and for digital transactions.
- **Interest:** if the outstanding amount on a credit card is **not paid on time, interest is charged**. The booklet notes interest does not apply to a debit card, because it spends the holder's own money.
- **Eligibility:** the booklet says a person's credit history is relevant for issuing a credit card, and not relevant for a debit card. For a debit card, having an account with the issuing bank is a prerequisite; for a credit card there is no compulsion to have an account with the issuer.
- Because a credit card is **borrowing**, spending that is not repaid in full by the due date can become expensive debt; a debit card cannot spend more than the account holds (unless an overdraft is arranged).
- **ATM and card safety (booklet):** change the PIN as soon as you receive the card; do not write the PIN down or disclose it to anyone; prefer ATMs within bank premises or those guarded round the clock, and use the ATM yourself, since the bank has no liability if the card was handed to someone else for the transaction; use the virtual keyboard for online transactions; and register for SMS alerts on transactions.
- If a card is lost or a transaction is not yours, **report it to the bank immediately** (see the unauthorised transactions article for the liability rules as the booklet describes them).
- The article does not describe fees, rewards or limits, which differ by bank and card and change over time.

## Worked Example

A hypothetical shopper buys a phone for ₹30,000.

- **With a debit card**, ₹30,000 leaves the savings account at once. If the balance was ₹50,000, ₹20,000 remains. There is nothing to repay.
- **With a credit card**, the bank lends ₹30,000 at the time of purchase. If the full amount is paid by the due date, no interest is charged on it (as the booklet's description implies). If it is not paid on time, interest is charged on the outstanding amount, so the real cost of the phone rises. The rate and the free period are set by the bank and are not stated here.

The figures are invented; check the card's terms for the actual charges.

## Misconceptions

- "A credit card is extra money." It is a loan that has to be repaid, with interest if not paid on time.
- "A debit card is safer for fraud in every case." Both can be misused; what matters is reporting quickly and not sharing PINs or OTPs.
- "I can let someone else use my card at an ATM." The booklet warns the bank has no liability if the card was handed to another person.
- "Credit and debit cards need the same eligibility." Credit history matters for a credit card, not for a debit card.

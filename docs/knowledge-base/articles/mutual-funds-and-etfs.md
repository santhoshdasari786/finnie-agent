# Mutual Funds and ETFs

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain what a mutual fund and an ETF are structurally, how their pricing/trading mechanics differ, and avoid presenting either as inherently "better" in general.

## Core Concepts

### Mutual Funds
- A mutual fund pools money from many investors and invests it according to a stated objective, managed by a fund manager/team. Per AMFI's own definition: "a mutual fund is a collective investment option where money from multiple investors... is pooled together and invested... managed by professional fund managers."
- Investors buy/sell **units**, priced once per day at the **NAV (Net Asset Value)**. AMFI's precise definition: NAV "is calculated at the end of each trading day after market closes by subtracting total liabilities from total assets and dividing by outstanding units."
- Can be **actively managed** (manager makes ongoing decisions attempting to beat a benchmark) or **passively managed/index funds** (tracking a benchmark index) — see "Index Funds vs. Actively Managed Funds."
- Bought/sold through the fund house (or a platform/distributor), not on a stock exchange, at end-of-day NAV.

### ETFs (Exchange-Traded Funds)
- Structurally similar to an index fund (usually tracks a benchmark), but **trades on a stock exchange like a share**, with a price that can fluctuate throughout the trading day. AMFI's own description: "ETF is a marketable security that tracks an index, a commodity, bonds, or a basket of assets like an index fund. ETFs are listed and traded on stock exchanges, with units compulsorily held in Demat mode."
- Priced continuously during market hours based on supply/demand, which can cause small deviations from the fund's actual underlying NAV (tracking difference/premium-discount) — unlike a mutual fund's single daily NAV.
- Typically requires a trading/demat account to buy and sell, and incurs brokerage/trading costs similar to buying a stock, in addition to the fund's own expense ratio.

### Key structural differences

| | Mutual Fund | ETF |
|---|---|---|
| Priced | Once daily (NAV) | Continuously during market hours |
| Bought via | Fund house / distributor / platform | Stock exchange, via a broker |
| Typical costs | Expense ratio (+ possible exit load) | Expense ratio + brokerage/trading costs |
| Minimum investment | Often allows small/SIP amounts | Priced per unit/share — effectively the unit's market price |

## Misconceptions

- "ETF" does not mean "automatically cheaper" — compare the actual expense ratio and trading costs, not just the product category (see "Expense Ratios and the Impact of Fees").
- A mutual fund's NAV and an ETF's trading price serve different purposes — an ETF's intraday price can briefly diverge from its underlying NAV, especially during volatile periods.
- Neither structure guarantees a specific return — both inherit the risk of whatever they invest in (equity, debt, etc.).

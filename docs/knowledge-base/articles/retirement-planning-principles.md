# Retirement Planning: Starting Early and Protecting the Corpus

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain the principles SEBI's booklet gives for retirement planning, show with numbers why time matters, and avoid recommending any product, amount or retirement age.

## Core Concepts

- SEBI's booklet notes that the move into retirement is rarely given the thought it deserves, and that without adequate planning a comfortable retirement probably will not happen.
- **Start early.** The booklet's illustration: someone who starts saving at 25 to retire at 60 can invest for 35 years. More years allow more time for returns to build on earlier returns.
- **Plan wisely:** set aside money for medical expenses and emergencies after retirement, and balance retirement saving against other goals such as children's education and marriage.
- **Track and review** the plan at regular intervals, and be comfortable with the risks, costs and liquidity of the investments chosen.
- **Don't dip into retirement savings** before retirement. The booklet warns that spending the retirement kitty on present needs leaves an inadequate corpus.
- Retirement savings run for decades, so **inflation** matters: a sum that looks large today buys less later. See the inflation worked example.
- Retirement income can come from several sources, such as government-run pension and provident schemes, employer schemes and personal investments. The booklet covers the National Pension System, which has its own article.
- A **time horizon** of decades usually means price swings matter less than steady saving and costs, though no investment is guaranteed; the horizon changes as retirement approaches.
- How much to save, what to hold, and when to retire depend on a person's income, expenses, other assets and health, which this article does not assess.

## Worked Example

A hypothetical comparison of two savers who each put ₹10,000 a month into an investment assumed to grow at 8% a year, compounded monthly. The rate is invented and not a forecast, and taxes and costs are ignored.

- **Saver A** starts at 25 and stops at 60: 35 years of saving.
- **Saver B** starts at 35 and stops at 60: 25 years of saving.

Computed in code: A ends with about ₹2.3 crore, B about ₹95 lakh. A paid in ₹42 lakh in total and B ₹30 lakh, so A paid in 40% more but ended with about 2.4 times as much, because the earlier contributions had ten more years to grow.

The result depends entirely on the assumed rate; with other rates the gap changes, and real returns vary year to year.

## Misconceptions

- "I can catch up later by saving more." Later money has less time to grow, so a larger amount may be needed, as the example shows.
- "Retirement savings are an emergency fund." The booklet advises against dipping into them before retirement.
- "A big corpus today will be enough in 30 years." Inflation reduces what it buys.
- "One plan lasts a lifetime." The booklet says to review the plan regularly.

# How to Verify a Financial Intermediary

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain why registration matters, say where in India registered intermediaries can be checked, list common red flags, and state clearly that being registered is necessary but not sufficient for trust.

## Core Concepts

- SEBI's Financial Education Booklet gives a firm rule: "Buying or selling of shares shall always be done through a **SEBI registered stock broker**", and "Don't deal with unregistered brokers / other unregistered intermediaries."
- **Where to check:** the booklet says "the list of SEBI registered stock brokers and depository participants may be obtained from SEBI's official website (www.sebi.gov.in) or from the websites of the respective stock exchanges and depositories". SEBI's website has an "Intermediaries / Market Infrastructure Institutions" section with a list of recognised intermediaries and lists of default or expelled stock brokers and terminated depository participants (page read on 2026-11-17).
- **Investment advisers** must be registered. SEBI's investor caution page says: "It is illegal to act as Investment Adviser without SEBI registration" and advises taking advice "only from entities registered under SEBI (Investment Adviser) Regulations, 2013". Advisers "should not handle cash or securities".
- **Malpractices SEBI lists** for registered and unregistered advisers: assured returns; charging exorbitant fees with a false promise of handsome returns; mis-selling that ignores the client's risk profile; trading on behalf of clients; and automatically moving clients to higher-risk products without consent.
- **Mutual fund distributors** are generally expected to hold a registration with AMFI (an ARN), and mutual funds themselves must be registered with SEBI before launching a scheme. The AMFI website is the place to check an ARN. (General knowledge; AMFI's lookup page was not read in this pass.)
- **Banks and NBFCs** are regulated by the RBI, and the RBI publishes lists of regulated entities on its website (general knowledge; not read in this pass).
- **Registration is a floor, not a guarantee.** A registered entity can still make mistakes, charge high fees or fail. SEBI keeps lists of defaulted and expelled brokers for this reason. Registration means the entity is supervised, not that it is endorsed.
- **Check the identity and the channel:** impostors copy real firms' names, logos and apps. Contact the firm using details from the regulator's list or its official site, not from a message or an advertisement.
- **Red flags** (consistent with SEBI's caution page): guaranteed or unusually steady returns; pressure to act quickly; being asked to send money to a personal account or to hand over passwords or OTPs; no registration number, or a number that does not match the name on the regulator's list.
- **Keep records:** written terms, contract notes and payment proofs, and always pay through banking channels (SEBI booklet).

## Worked Example

A hypothetical message offers "guaranteed 5% monthly returns" and asks the recipient to pay into a personal account. A cautious check might go:

1. Ask for the sender's registration number and category (broker, adviser, research analyst).
2. Look the name up on the regulator's list of registered intermediaries, not on the sender's own page.
3. Notice that no registered entity may promise assured returns (SEBI lists assured returns as a malpractice) and that payments should go to the intermediary's registered account through banking channels.
4. If the name is missing, or the details do not match, treat the offer as unregistered and stop. A complaint can be filed on SCORES (see "Investor Grievance Channels").

The example describes the verification steps. It does not say any specific firm is safe or unsafe.

## Misconceptions

- "A registration certificate on a website proves it." Check the regulator's own list; certificates can be copied.
- "Registered means guaranteed." It means supervised, not safe from loss.
- "An app on an app store must be legitimate." Fake trading apps are a known scam (SEBI has a page warning about them).
- "Only shares need a registered intermediary." Advisers, mutual fund distributors, brokers and depository participants are all covered, each by its own rules.

# Unclaimed Shares and Dividends and the Investor Education and Protection Fund

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain what IEPF is, what happens to dividends and shares that are not claimed, and how an investor or a family can try to recover them, while noting that the time period and procedure are set by law and should be checked.

## Core Concepts

- The **Investor Education and Protection Fund (IEPF)** was established under Section 125 of the Companies Act, 2013, to promote investor awareness and protect investors' interests. The **IEPF Authority** is a statutory body constituted under that Act (BSE booklet).
- According to the booklet, **dividends and shares that remain unpaid or unclaimed for seven consecutive years** are transferred by the company to the IEPF Authority. Verify the current period, as it is set by law.
- Money or shares can be unclaimed for ordinary reasons: a **changed address or bank account** that was not updated, a **shareholder who has died** without nominees or heirs coming forward, lost paperwork, or investments an investor has forgotten.
- Being transferred to IEPF **does not mean the investor loses the right**. The investor or their representatives **submit a claim to the IEPF Authority** to receive the unpaid dividend or unclaimed shares. The booklet points to the Authority's refund page for the process.
- The practical protections are the same habits elsewhere in the materials: **keep contact and bank details updated** with the company's registrar, broker and depository participant, **register nominations**, and **keep records** of holdings and statements.
- Families dealing with a deceased person's investments are often the ones who find unclaimed holdings, so keeping a list of accounts and who to contact is useful (see the estate planning article).
- Other official tools for tracing forgotten investments exist, such as one for mutual fund folios (SEBI's website names a platform called MITRA for unclaimed mutual fund folios; its page was not read for this article), so check SEBI's investor website for the current list.
- Process details, forms and timelines vary and change; confirm them on the IEPF Authority's website.

## Worked Example

A hypothetical investor held shares in a company, moved house and did not update the address with the registrar. Dividends were posted to an old bank account that was later closed, and the payments came back unpaid year after year.

1. After the stated number of consecutive years, the company transfers the unpaid dividends, and eventually the shares, to the IEPF Authority.
2. The investor later finds the old holding on a statement or in a family record.
3. To recover them, the investor, or a legal heir with the required papers, applies to the IEPF Authority through its claim process and provides proof of identity and holding.
4. Doing the updates earlier (address, bank details, nomination) would have avoided the transfer.

The scenario is invented and the exact claim steps must be taken from the Authority's website.

## Misconceptions

- "If my shares go to IEPF, they are gone." They can be claimed from the Authority by the investor or their representatives.
- "Unclaimed dividends are only a problem for old investors." Anyone who changes address or bank accounts without updating records can miss payments.
- "The company will keep chasing me." The booklet describes a transfer after a long period of non-payment, so the investor must claim.
- "A nomination is not needed if I have a will." Both help; neither replaces keeping records current.

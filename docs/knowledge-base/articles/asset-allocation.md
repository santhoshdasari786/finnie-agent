# What Is Asset Allocation?

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain what asset allocation is, how it differs from diversification (a related but distinct idea), and why it's often described as the single biggest driver of a portfolio's overall risk/return profile.

## Core Concepts

- **Asset allocation** is the decision of how much of a portfolio to put into each broad asset class — equity, debt/fixed income, cash, and others (gold, real estate, etc.). The SEC's own Beginners' Guide calls this, in some experts' view, "the most important decision" an investor makes — more consequential than picking individual securities.
- It's distinct from **diversification**, which is about spreading *within* an asset class (e.g. owning many different stocks) — a portfolio can be diversified within equities while still being 100% equity, i.e. not allocated across asset classes at all. The same SEC guide states this explicitly: "choosing an asset allocation model won't necessarily diversify your portfolio" — allocation and diversification are related but separate decisions.
- Allocation is typically driven by: time horizon, risk tolerance/capacity, and the specific goal the money is earmarked for. Per the guide: stocks offer the highest growth potential but the most short-term volatility; bonds are less volatile with more modest returns; cash is safest but has the lowest returns and carries its own inflation risk.
- A common framing: equity-heavy allocations suit longer horizons and higher risk tolerance; debt/cash-heavy allocations suit shorter horizons or lower risk tolerance — but there's no single "correct" allocation for everyone, and the guide notes the right mix changes at different points in an investor's life.
- Allocation isn't a one-time decision — it typically needs periodic review as time horizon shortens or goals change (see "What Is Portfolio Rebalancing?").

## Worked Example

Illustrative allocation patterns only — not a recommendation for any specific person:

| Illustrative profile | Equity | Debt | Cash |
|---|---|---|---|
| Longer horizon, higher risk tolerance | 70% | 25% | 5% |
| Moderate horizon/tolerance | 50% | 40% | 10% |
| Shorter horizon, lower risk tolerance | 25% | 60% | 15% |

These are illustrative patterns for explaining the *concept*, not a personalized recommendation — actual allocation decisions depend on an individual's full financial situation.

## Misconceptions

- Asset allocation and diversification are often used interchangeably but describe different things — allocation is *across* classes, diversification is *within* them.
- There's no universally "correct" allocation — it depends on horizon, goals, and risk capacity, which vary by person.
- Allocation set once and never revisited can drift significantly over time purely due to different asset classes growing at different rates (see "What Is Portfolio Rebalancing?").

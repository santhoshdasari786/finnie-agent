# Government Social Security Schemes: Jan Dhan, Insurance and Pension Schemes

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain in plain words what each major government social-security scheme is for, how it is usually accessed, and why eligibility, premiums and benefits must be confirmed from official sources.

## Core Concepts

- **Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY):** launched in August 2014 for financial inclusion, so that every individual without a bank account can have one, with access to banking, savings and deposits, remittance, credit, insurance and pension. Accounts can be opened at any bank branch or through a business correspondent (Bank Mitra). Features listed in the booklet include a zero-balance account, a RuPay debit card, accident insurance cover, an overdraft facility up to a certain amount on one account per household, mobile banking, and direct benefit transfer of government payments.
- **Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana:** accident insurance for bank account holders in a stated age group, with a fixed annual premium auto-debited from the account; it covers accidental death and permanent or partial disability, and a person can join through one savings account only.
- **Pradhan Mantri Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana:** life insurance cover for bank account holders in a stated age group, with a fixed annual premium auto-debited from the bank account, joined through one savings account only.
- **Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (Ayushman Bharat):** health coverage aimed at poor and deprived rural families and identified urban worker categories. Eligible families are identified from the Socio-Economic Caste Census 2011 database; no payment is needed by the family for covered hospital treatment, and pre-existing conditions are covered from day one, at public or empanelled private hospitals.
- **Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana:** crop insurance that pays based on the gap between a threshold yield and the actual yield after a crop loss; it is compulsory for farmers who have institutional loans and covers risks such as drought, floods, pests and cyclones.
- **Atal Pension Yojana (APY):** a pension-oriented savings scheme for unorganised-sector workers who are not covered by other social security schemes; it can be opened by bank account holders within a stated age range, and exit before age 60 is generally not permitted except for death or terminal illness, though a spouse can continue.
- Most of these work **through a bank account**, with premiums or contributions auto-debited, so keeping the account funded and the contact details current matters.
- Eligibility, age limits, premiums, cover amounts, and whether a scheme is still open change over time; the booklet's description is from around 2020.
- These schemes are **not investments**: they are protection or pension arrangements, and their benefits are set by the scheme.

## Worked Example

A hypothetical household with a Jan Dhan account wants to understand what is available.

1. The account is a basic account; the booklet lists a zero-balance feature and a RuPay debit card.
2. They look up whether the accident and life insurance schemes are open to them, and what the current premium and cover are, on the bank's or the government's official page.
3. A family member in the unorganised sector looks at the pension scheme's current age range and contribution table before deciding anything.
4. They note the auto-debit date and keep enough in the account, since a failed debit can lapse the cover.

The steps show what to check; they do not say who is eligible or what any benefit is today.

## Misconceptions

- "These schemes are an investment that grows." They give cover or pension according to rules, not market returns.
- "Anyone can join any scheme." Each has eligibility conditions such as age, income category or occupation.
- "Once joined, cover continues no matter what." Auto-debit must succeed, and scheme terms can change.
- "The booklet's figures are today's." They were current around 2020; check official sources.

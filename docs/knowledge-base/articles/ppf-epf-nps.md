# PPF, EPF and NPS: Long-Term Savings Schemes

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to describe what each of the three schemes is for, how it is structured at a high level, who regulates it, and why any specific rate, limit or withdrawal share must be taken from the current official source and not from this article.

## Critical Constraint

**Interest rates, contribution limits, exit rules and tax treatment of these schemes are changed by the Government, the EPFO or PFRDA, and have changed more than once.** The worked example below shows the same NPS rule appearing differently in sources of different dates. When a question needs a number, point the user to the current NSI, EPFO or PFRDA page.

## Core Concepts

- **Public Provident Fund (PPF)** is a government-backed long-term savings scheme run through banks and post offices under the Public Provident Fund Scheme. It matures after **15 complete financial years** and can be extended in blocks of five years, with or without further deposits (NSI scheme page, via search summary). There is a minimum and maximum deposit per financial year, an interest rate that the Government announces periodically, and rules on loans and part-withdrawals that start only after several years. Check the current limits and rate.
- **Employees' Provident Fund (EPF)** is a retirement savings scheme for salaried employees, run by the EPFO. The employee contributes a percentage of basic wages and dearness allowance, and the employer contributes too; part of the employer's share goes to the Employees' Pension Scheme rather than to the provident fund account (EPFO FAQ, via search summary). The EPFO declares the interest rate each year. A UAN identifies the member across employers.
- **National Pension System (NPS)** is a market-linked retirement scheme regulated by **PFRDA**. Money is invested through a pension fund manager into equity, government bonds, corporate debt and similar classes, either by the subscriber's choice (Active) or by an age-based plan (Auto) (SEBI Financial Education Booklet and Beginner's Guide). A Permanent Retirement Account Number (PRAN) stays with the subscriber.
- **NPS has tiers:** Tier I is the long-term retirement account with restrictions on withdrawal; Tier II is a voluntary account without such restrictions and without the same tax benefits (PFRDA FAQs, via search summary).
- **At retirement** an NPS subscriber must use part of the corpus to buy an **annuity** from an insurer, and may take the rest as a lump sum, subject to PFRDA's current rules. The split has changed over time.
- **Risk differs.** PPF and EPF interest is declared by the Government or EPFO, so rupee returns are set in advance for the year; NPS returns depend on markets and can rise or fall.
- **Regulators differ:** NPS is regulated by PFRDA; EPF by the EPFO under the Ministry of Labour; PPF by the Government under the PPF Scheme (see "Understanding India's Financial Regulators").
- **Tax treatment** of contributions, interest and withdrawals differs between the three and between tax regimes, and changes with the law (see "Pre-Tax vs. Post-Tax Returns").

## Worked Example

The same NPS exit question, answered by sources of different dates, shows why not to quote a figure from memory:

| Source | What it says about the share that must buy an annuity at 60 |
|---|---|
| SEBI Beginner's Guide / Financial Education Booklet (about 2020) | at least 40%, the rest withdrawable |
| PFRDA exit FAQ page read on 2026-11-17 (older text on the page) | minimum 40% for the annuity |
| A newer PFRDA "All Citizen" summary seen in a search result | at least 20% annuity, with different options for small corpuses |

The numbers differ because the rule changed. A correct answer today needs the current PFRDA circular. These figures are listed to show the pattern and are not a statement of today's rule.

## Misconceptions

- "These three schemes work the same way." They differ in who runs them, how returns are set, and how and when money can be withdrawn.
- "Last year's rate applies this year." PPF and EPF rates are set periodically; NPS returns follow markets.
- "NPS returns are guaranteed like PPF." NPS is market-linked.
- "PPF is only for people without an employer." PPF is open to individuals generally; EPF is tied to employment.

# Trading Days, Pay-in, Pay-out and the T+N Settlement Cycle

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain when shares can be traded, what pay-in and pay-out days are, how to read T+N, and why a settlement cycle quoted in an older document must be verified, without describing a current timetable as fact.

## Core Concepts

- SEBI's booklet says trading on the stock exchange takes place on all days of the week except Saturdays, Sundays and holidays that the exchange announces in advance.
- A **trade day** is "T". The **settlement cycle** is written as T+N, meaning the trade is settled N working days after the trade day.
- On a purchase, the investor pays the broker before the pay-in day; after pay-out, the broker credits the shares to the investor's demat account.
- On a sale, the investor delivers the shares to the broker's demat account before pay-in; after pay-out, the broker credits the money to the investor's bank account.
- **Pay-in day:** the day the broker pays funds (buy) or delivers securities (sale) to the exchange side of the system. **Pay-out day:** the day the exchange pays funds or delivers securities to the broker (SEBI booklet).
- The booklet states that the broker must pass on pay-out to the client within 24 hours.
- The booklet describes a **T+2 rolling settlement** from April 2003, with an example that a Monday trade settles on Wednesday. The how-stock-exchanges-work article records that exchanges later moved equity to a shorter cycle; that is only a search-summary finding there, so treat any cycle as something to verify.
- A shorter cycle means money and shares move sooner; it does not change what a trade is or the price at which it was done.
- Exchange holidays and weekends are skipped in counting working days, so a trade before a holiday can settle later than the calendar suggests.
- Funds not arriving on time, or insufficient shares for a sale, can lead to penalties (BSE booklet), so check the broker's cut-off times.

## Worked Example

A hypothetical investor buys shares on a Monday under a T+2 cycle, as the booklet describes.

- Trade day (T): Monday. Pay-in and pay-out fall on T+2: Wednesday.
- The shares are available in the demat account after pay-out, and the broker must pass them on within 24 hours of that pay-out.
- If Tuesday were an exchange holiday, T+2 would be counted over working days only and fall on Thursday.
- Under a T+1 cycle the same Monday trade would settle on Tuesday (or Wednesday if Tuesday were a holiday).

The dates are invented for illustration; confirm the cycle in force with the exchange or broker.

## Misconceptions

- "Trading happens every day." It does not happen on weekends and announced holidays.
- "T+2 means two calendar days." It counts working days.
- "Money from a sale is in the bank the moment the trade executes." Settlement happens at pay-out, followed by the broker's transfer.
- "The settlement cycle never changes." The booklet's T+2 is dated; check current rules.

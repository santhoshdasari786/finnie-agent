# What Is Compounding?

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain how compounding differs from simple interest, why the *rate* and *time horizon* both matter more than either one alone, and walk through a worked numeric example without improvising the arithmetic.

## Core Concepts

- **Simple interest** grows only on the original principal — the interest earned each period is constant in absolute terms.
- **Compound interest** grows on the principal *plus* all previously accumulated interest/returns — each period's base is larger than the last.
- **Compounding frequency** (annual, monthly, daily) changes the effective growth rate — more frequent compounding on the same stated annual rate produces slightly higher total growth.
- **Time horizon** has an outsized effect: the longest-held portion of a compounding investment contributes disproportionately to the final value, because it has had the most periods to compound.
- Compounding applies to debt (interest owed) as much as to investments (returns earned) — the same mechanic, opposite direction.

## Worked Example

A hypothetical ₹10,000 investment growing at an **assumed** 8% annually, compounded yearly, with no additional contributions, fees, or taxes:

| Year | Value |
|---|---|
| 0 | ₹10,000 |
| 5 | ₹14,693 |
| 10 | ₹21,589 |
| 20 | ₹46,610 |

Note how the growth from year 10→20 (≈₹25,000) is larger than the entire growth from year 0→10 (≈₹11,589) — that's compounding, not a changing rate.

*(Assumption: constant 8% annual return, no volatility, no withdrawals. Real investments do not grow in a straight line.)*

### A Second, Officially-Sourced Example: Cost of Delay

AMFI's own investor education booklet gives a real-world illustration of the same mechanic applied to starting early: two hypothetical investors each invest a total of ₹21 lakh, assuming a 10% annual return with monthly compounding, and both stay invested until age 60.

| | Starts at age | Monthly investment | Years invested | Ending corpus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Friend 1 | 25 | ₹5,000 | 35 | ≈ ₹1.91 crore |
| Friend 2 | 35 | ₹7,000 | 25 | ≈ ₹93.65 lakh |

Same total amount invested (₹21 lakh), very different outcomes — purely from the extra 10 years of compounding. AMFI's own disclaimer applies here too: "the calculations shown are for informational and illustrative purposes only."

## Misconceptions

- An assumed rate (like the 8% above) is **not** a guaranteed return — real markets fluctuate year to year even if long-run averages land near an assumed figure.
- Compounding does not eliminate investment risk — it describes *how* growth accumulates, not *whether* growth happens.
- "Compound interest" isn't unique to any one product — it applies to fixed deposits, mutual funds, bonds, and even credit card debt alike.

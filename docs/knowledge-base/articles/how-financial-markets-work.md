# How Financial Markets Work

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain why financial markets exist, the difference between the primary and secondary market, who the main participants are, and what role a regulator plays, using plain language and no price predictions.

## Core Concepts

- A business that needs more money than its owner and friends can supply can raise it **from the public by issuing securities**, for example shares. SEBI's Beginner's Guide describes how "even small amounts available with a very large number of individuals translate into usable capital for corporates". This mechanism is the **primary market**.
- The **secondary market** is where those securities are traded afterwards. When a shareholder sells to another investor, the trade "does not reduce or alter the company's capital" (SEBI Beginner's Guide). Stock exchanges bring buyers and sellers together, which is why a company raising money from the public must list its shares.
- In India's **primary market**, a company issuing shares for the first time through an initial public offer (IPO) prepares a prospectus with merchant bankers, and SEBI examines it against its regulations (SEBI Financial Education Booklet).
- The **secondary market** in India is mainly the BSE and the NSE, which SEBI recognises as stock exchanges (same booklet). Anyone can buy securities there if they will pay the price at which they trade.
- **Equity** (ownership) and **debt** (loans) are the two basic claims. Hybrids combine features of both. Mutual funds let many investors pool money into a mix of these (see "Asset Classes: An Overview").
- Between savers and companies stand many **intermediaries** (brokers, depositories, mutual funds, merchant bankers, registrars). The securities a company offers may not match what a saver wants, so intermediaries bring the two sides together (SEBI Beginner's Guide).
- A **regulator** supervises the market. SEBI "ensures supply of quality securities and non-manipulated demand for them", develops best practices and takes enforcement action against wrongdoers (SEBI Beginner's Guide).

## Worked Example

A hypothetical company needs money to build a factory. Illustration only:

1. **Primary market:** it issues new shares to the public through an IPO. Investors apply; the company receives the money for the shares it issues.
2. **Listing:** the shares begin trading on an exchange.
3. **Secondary market:** an investor who bought shares in the IPO later sells them to another investor through a broker. The seller gets cash; the buyer gets the shares; **the company receives nothing from this second trade**, because it is between investors.

## Misconceptions

- "Buying a share on the exchange gives money to the company." Only the primary issue does. Everyday exchange trades are between investors.
- "The market is one big pool where prices only go up." Prices are set by buying and selling, and they fall as well as rise.
- "The regulator guarantees investments." SEBI regulates intermediaries and disclosure and enforces rules; it does not guarantee returns or protect against losses from price movements.

# Asset Classes: An Overview

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to name the main asset classes, say in one or two sentences what each one is and where its risk comes from, and explain that mixing them is a design choice, not a ranking of "good" and "bad" assets.

## Core Concepts

- An **asset class** is a group of investments that behave similarly. The common ones in Indian retail investing are equity, debt, hybrid, and commodities such as gold.
- **Equity** is ownership. SEBI's Beginner's Guide: equity shares represent "the title to the ownership of a company", and the holder "bears the entrepreneurial risk of the business venture" while being entitled to benefits such as dividends. Returns depend on the company's profits and growth, so they can be high but also uncertain.
- **Debt** is lending. A debenture or bond pays interest at a fixed rate on set dates and repays the loan at a specified maturity (SEBI Beginner's Guide). Government securities are issued by the Government of India and, being sovereign borrowing, are "free from risk of default (credit risk)", though their prices still move with interest rates.
- **Money market instruments** are short-term loans up to one year, such as treasury bills, commercial papers and certificates of deposit (same guide).
- **Hybrid** instruments combine ownership and loan features, for example preference shares. Hybrid mutual funds hold a mix of equity and debt.
- **Mutual funds** are not an asset class themselves but a way to hold the classes above. SEBI's guide lists equity funds, bond/income funds, balanced funds, money market funds and others.
- **Gold and other commodities** are real assets. They produce no interest or dividend; returns come from price changes.
- Each class has a different mix of **return potential, risk, liquidity and taxation**. SEBI's Financial Education Booklet lists seven risks, including market, inflation, liquidity, business, volatility and currency risk.

## Worked Example

An illustrative comparison. Labels describe typical *tendencies*, not guarantees:

| Asset class | Main source of return | Main risk | Typical liquidity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equity shares | Company profit growth, dividends | Large price swings; company failure | High on an exchange |
| Government bonds | Fixed interest | Price falls when rates rise | High if traded; can be held to maturity |
| Bank fixed deposit | Fixed interest | Inflation; early-exit penalty | Lower; fixed term |
| Gold | Price changes | Price swings; no income | Depends on the form held |

## Misconceptions

- "Debt is risk-free." Government debt carries no default risk, but all debt has interest-rate risk, and corporate debt also has credit risk (see "Credit Risk and Credit Ratings").
- "Equity always beats the others over any period." SEBI's guide says higher risk means higher *potential* return, and adds: "There is no guarantee."
- "More asset classes always means safer." Mixing classes can reduce concentration, but each asset still carries its own risks.

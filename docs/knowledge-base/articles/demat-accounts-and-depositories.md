# Demat Accounts, Depositories and Depository Participants

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain what a demat account is, who holds the investor's securities, how a depository differs from a depository participant, and what the account does for the investor, without recommending a particular provider.

## Core Concepts

- A **depository** is an entity that holds securities (shares, bonds, mutual fund units and so on) in electronic, or dematerialised, form and makes it smooth to transfer them between buyers and sellers. SEBI's investor site says it removes the risks of physical securities such as theft, forgery and mutilation.
- India has two depositories, **NSDL** (established 1996) and **CDSL** (established 1999), both regulated by SEBI under the Depositories Act, 1996.
- A **depository participant (DP)** is the intermediary the investor deals with: a bank, stockbroker or financial institution registered with SEBI and affiliated to NSDL or CDSL. Investors cannot open an account directly with NSDL or CDSL.
- A **demat account** is the account, opened with a DP, in which securities are held electronically, similar to how a bank account holds money. It is separate from a trading account used to place orders, though many brokers offer both together.
- **Dematerialisation** converts physical certificates into electronic form; **rematerialisation** converts them back if required.
- **Transfers and settlement** happen through book entries, so there is no physical movement of paper. Clearing corporations guarantee the settlement of trades and work with the depositories to move securities from sellers to buyers.
- **Pledging:** securities in a demat account can be pledged as collateral for a loan or margin, on the investor's instruction.
- **Corporate actions** such as dividends, bonus issues, splits and rights are credited to the account through the depository system, with registrars and transfer agents handling actions like dividend distribution and share allotment.
- **Nomination:** investors can name nominees for the account (see the nomination article, as the rules change).
- **Complaints:** SEBI's page says a DP must resolve complaints within 21 days, after which the matter can be escalated to the depository and to SEBI through SCORES. Verify the current timeline when a complaint matters.

## Worked Example

A hypothetical investor buys 10 shares through a broker.

1. The order executes on the exchange through the broker.
2. The clearing corporation arranges settlement of the trade.
3. The shares are credited to the investor's demat account held with their DP, which is affiliated to NSDL or CDSL. The investor sees them as electronic holdings, not paper certificates.
4. Later, a bonus issue is declared: the extra shares are credited to the same account automatically through the depository system.

The steps are a simplified description of the flow; the timing of settlement depends on the exchange's current rules.

## Misconceptions

- "NSDL or CDSL holds my account directly." The account is opened with a depository participant, and the depository records and safeguards the securities.
- "A demat account and a trading account are the same." One holds securities; the other is for placing orders.
- "A demat account holds money." It holds securities; money sits in a bank account.
- "Moving a broker means selling my shares." Securities can be transferred between demat accounts, including between the two depositories, without selling (SEBI FAQ on the depository system, seen as a search excerpt).

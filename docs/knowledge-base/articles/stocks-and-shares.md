# Stocks and Shares

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain what a share is, how a shareholder can earn from it and lose on it, what market capitalisation means, and what accounts are needed to buy shares in India, without recommending any company.

## Core Concepts

- **Equity shares** are issued by companies to raise capital and represent "the title to the ownership of a company" (SEBI Beginner's Guide). A shareholder is a part-owner and "bears the entrepreneurial risk of the business venture".
- A shareholder can earn in two ways: **dividends**, a company's profit distributed to owners, and **price changes**, selling for more than the purchase price. The reverse is also possible: SEBI's guide says of price gains "provided you make the right choice. The reverse is also true!"
- The **returns depend on the company's profits** and future growth (SEBI Beginner's Guide), so they are uncertain. A dividend is not owed to the shareholder in the way interest is owed to a lender.
- **Market capitalisation** is "the value of a corporation determined by multiplying the current public market price of one share of the corporation by the number of total outstanding shares" (SEC Investor.gov). Companies are commonly grouped as large-, mid- or small-cap by this size; the cut-offs are set by market rules and can change.
- **Preference shares** are a hybrid: a fixed dividend paid before equity shareholders, and a higher claim than equity holders (but below creditors) if the company is wound up (SEBI Beginner's Guide).
- In India, buying shares on an exchange needs three accounts: a **savings (bank) account**, a **trading account** with a SEBI-registered stock broker, and a **demat account** with a SEBI-recognised depository participant to hold the shares electronically (SEBI Financial Education Booklet). NSDL and CDSL are the two depositories.
- Shares are bought in the **primary market** (a company's IPO) or the **secondary market** (on a stock exchange from other investors).
- The main risks, per SEBI: market risk, company-specific risk, volatility, liquidity and business risk (see "Volatility vs. Permanent Loss").

## Worked Example

A hypothetical company has 10 crore shares outstanding and the market price is ₹250 per share.

- Market capitalisation = ₹250 × 10 crore shares = **₹2,500 crore**.
- If the price moves to ₹275, market capitalisation becomes ₹2,750 crore even though nothing about the company's ownership changed.
- If the company pays a dividend of ₹5 per share, a holder of 100 shares receives ₹500 (before any tax). That payment is declared by the company and is not guaranteed in future years.

## Misconceptions

- "A cheap share price means a cheap company." A low price per share says nothing about value. Market capitalisation, not price per share, shows a company's size.
- "Buying a share means lending money to the company." Shareholders are owners, not lenders; lenders (bondholders) are paid before shareholders if the company fails.
- "Shares always pay dividends." Dividends are optional distributions that depend on profits and the board's decision.
- "The exchange or SEBI guarantees the share's price." They regulate trading; they do not protect against price falls.

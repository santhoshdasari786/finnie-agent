# Basic Services Demat Account (BSDA): A Lower-Cost Account for Small Holdings

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain what a BSDA is, who it is meant for and how its charges differ in structure from a regular demat account, while saying the thresholds and charges are set by SEBI and the depositories and should be checked.

## Core Concepts

- The **Basic Services Demat Account (BSDA)** is a demat account facility provided to **individuals who have only one demat account across all depositories** and whose **value of holdings does not exceed stated limits** at any time (BSE booklet). Separate limits apply to non-debt securities such as equities and to debt securities.
- Its purpose is **wider financial inclusion**: lower annual costs for small investors.
- The **annual maintenance charge (AMC)** structure is tiered by the value of holdings: below the first threshold there is **no AMC**, and above it up to the ceiling for BSDA the charge is capped at a small stated amount. Beyond the BSDA limit, an account no longer qualifies and is treated as a regular account.
- The booklet's amounts and thresholds are from around 2020 and have been revised since; they are **not quoted here** because they change. Check the current figures with SEBI or the depository.
- A BSDA still carries the **same basics** as any demat account: holdings are electronic, the investor deals through a depository participant, and KYC applies.
- How an account is categorised and what happens when holdings cross the limit are set under SEBI rules and the depository participant's practice; confirm both with the participant.
- Charges on a demat account are separate from **brokerage and other trading charges**, which depend on the broker.
- Having more than one demat account in a person's name can stop a person qualifying for a BSDA, so check across depositories before relying on it.
- Always get the **tariff schedule** in writing from the depository participant and compare accounts on the whole set of charges, not on one fee.

## Worked Example

A hypothetical investor has one demat account with holdings that are small compared with the BSDA limit.

1. They confirm with the depository participant that they hold no other demat account anywhere, since eligibility is across all depositories.
2. They ask for the BSDA tariff in writing and check the holding value at which the account stops qualifying.
3. As their holdings grow past the limit, the account is treated as a regular account and the charges rise to the regular schedule, so they review the new tariff.

The steps show what to confirm; no figures are given because they change.

## Misconceptions

- "A BSDA has no costs." The charge is lower, and only below a threshold is there no AMC.
- "Any investor can get a BSDA." Eligibility depends on holding only one demat account and keeping holdings within limits.
- "The booklet's limits are current." They were set around 2020 and have been revised.
- "A BSDA changes how safe my holdings are." It changes the charge structure, not the depository system.

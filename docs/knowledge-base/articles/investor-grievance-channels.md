# Investor Grievances and Official Complaint Channels

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain the usual order of escalation for a complaint, name the official channels for securities, banking, insurance and pension matters, list what to keep as records, and tell the user to confirm contact details and time limits on the official sites.

## Critical Constraint

Portals, helpline numbers, time limits and ombudsman schemes are revised from time to time (for example, the RBI portal now lists its Integrated Ombudsman Scheme under a 2026 title). This article gives the **order of steps and where to look**, not figures. Confirm current details on the regulator's own site.

## Core Concepts

- **Step 1: complain to the company or intermediary first.** SEBI's booklet: "Whenever an individual entity faces an issue, he or she has to first contact his or her company, exchange or broker for filing his complaint." Do it in writing and keep the acknowledgement.
- **Step 2: escalate to the regulator.** If the reply is unsatisfactory or absent, the investor can lodge a complaint on **SCORES**, SEBI's complaint redress system, for matters in the securities market (listed companies and registrars, brokers and exchanges, depository participants and depositories, mutual funds and other entities). The SCORES home page states that grievances sent by email to SEBI addresses "will not be attended to / entertained" and must be lodged on the portal.
- **Time limit:** SEBI's booklet says a complaint can be lodged on SCORES "within three years from the date of cause of complaint". Confirm the current time limit on SCORES.
- **What happens:** SEBI examines whether the matter falls within its purview, and if so forwards it to the entity with a request for a written reply and an action-taken report. Each complaint gets a registration number to track it.
- **What SCORES does not take:** SEBI's list of non-complaints includes incomplete or vague complaints, allegations without supporting documents, requests for suggestions, dissatisfaction with a share's trading price or illiquidity, and disputes from private agreements with companies.
- **Helpline:** SEBI's booklet lists toll-free helpline numbers (1800 266 7575 and 1800 22 7575) that give guidance but "do not offer any legal opinion or investment advice". Numbers may have changed; confirm on SEBI's site.
- **Dispute resolution online:** a securities-market online dispute resolution portal exists (SMART ODR, whose page title was read; its content did not render for automated reading), and exchanges and depositories also run arbitration.
- **Banks and NBFCs:** complaints go first to the bank or institution, then to the RBI's complaint management system and its Integrated Ombudsman Scheme (RBI portal, read 2026-11-17); a contact centre number is shown on the portal.
- **Insurance** → IRDAI; **pension (NPS)** → PFRDA; **deposits of NBFCs and Nidhi companies** → RBI; **company deposits** → Ministry of Corporate Affairs; **chit funds and co-operative societies** → state governments (SEBI booklet, Chapter 13). IRDAI and PFRDA channels were not read in this pass.
- **Keep records:** the complaint text, dates, reference numbers, replies, contract notes, statements and payment proofs. Keep the complaint specific and attach supporting documents.

## Worked Example

A hypothetical mis-sold investment, described in steps without naming a firm:

1. The investor writes to the intermediary stating the facts, the amount, the dates and what outcome is asked for, and keeps proof of sending.
2. If there is no satisfactory reply within a reasonable time, the investor registers on SCORES, selects the right category (for example, mutual funds or brokers), and attaches the documents.
3. The investor tracks the complaint with the registration number and responds to any request for more information.
4. If the matter is outside SEBI's purview (for example a bank product), the investor uses the relevant regulator's channel instead.

Whether a complaint succeeds depends on the facts and the rules. The steps simply organise the process.

## Misconceptions

- "Complaining to the regulator first is faster." The regulator expects you to have approached the entity first.
- "Email the regulator." SCORES says emails to SEBI addresses will not be attended; use the portal.
- "A complaint recovers my loss." It starts a process; the outcome varies.
- "There is no deadline." Time limits exist; act promptly.

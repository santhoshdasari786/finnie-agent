# Protecting Your Investment Accounts and Financial Data

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to give practical, widely accepted safety habits for banking and investing accounts, explain how common fraud works, and describe the quick-reporting steps, without asking for or handling any user's credentials.

## Core Concepts

- **Never share secrets.** SEBI's booklet: "Never share your password for online account with anyone. Do change the passwords frequently." The same applies to PINs, OTPs and card details. A genuine bank, broker or regulator does not need them from you (general guidance).
- **Use the official channel.** SEBI's digital-payments tips: "Always visit your bank's secure Internet Banking website directly", avoid internet banking on unsecured Wi-Fi in public places, do not install apps "from untrustworthy and unreliable sources", and keep the phone and app updated.
- **Protect the device:** use a password or lock on your phone and computer; "never leave your handset unattended and logged into a mobile banking app"; log out after each transaction.
- **Do not save banking logins on the phone**, and do not ignore your balance statements and transaction history (SEBI booklet).
- **Keep contact details current.** SEBI's do's include informing your broker or depository participant of changes to address, bank details, email or mobile number, and keeping the same mobile number linked to accounts, because "mobile number is the key to all important transactions". Register for SMS and email alerts.
- **Check trades and statements.** Review the exchange's daily SMS and email on trades and the monthly statement of funds and securities. Insist on a contract note.
- **Phishing and "request money" tricks.** The RBI complaint portal warns: "Never respond to 'Request money' options from unknown sources. A fraudster may not ask you to transfer money directly but click on a 'request money' or other such email and SMS links."
- **Do not sign blank forms or Delivery Instruction Slips, and do not give a general power of attorney** to a broker or depository participant (SEBI booklet).
- **Fake apps and impostors:** SEBI has a page warning against fake trading apps. Download apps only from the official source named by a registered entity, and verify the entity first (see "How to Verify a Financial Intermediary").
- **If something goes wrong, report at once.** The SEBI booklet describes the RBI rule on customer liability: a customer has no liability for an unauthorised transaction caused by fraud or the bank's deficiency, and, where the fault lies elsewhere in the system, no liability if the customer notifies the bank within three days of the alert; if the customer's negligence caused the loss, the customer bears it until reported. Check the current RBI directions, which may have been updated since the 2020 booklet.
- **Reporting channels:** your bank first; the RBI's contact centre (shown on the RBI complaint portal) for banking complaints; and the national cybercrime reporting portal and helpline for cyber fraud (the helpline number and portal were not read in this pass; confirm on cybercrime.gov.in).

## Worked Example

A hypothetical case showing why speed matters, described in steps:

1. A caller claiming to be from a bank asks for an OTP "to stop a suspicious transaction". The customer shares it.
2. Money leaves the account within minutes. A genuine bank would not ask for the OTP; the customer's sharing counts as the customer's negligence under the rule described above.
3. The customer calls the bank at once, asks it to block the card and account, and files a complaint on the cybercrime portal. Reporting within the stated window is what protects the customer's position under the customer-liability rules; delay weakens it.

The example is a safety illustration, not legal advice on liability in any case.

## Misconceptions

- "A call from my bank can be trusted if the caller knows my name." Scammers use leaked details; use the number on your card or the bank's site.
- "OTPs are only for me to read." OTPs authorise payments, so sharing one hands over the money.
- "Only large accounts get targeted." Frauds are automated and target accounts of every size.
- "A reported fraud is automatically refunded." Outcomes depend on the facts and the time of reporting.

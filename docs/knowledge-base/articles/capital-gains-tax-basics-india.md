# Capital Gains Tax Basics in India

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain the *structure* of how capital gains are taxed in India — that classification depends on holding period and asset type, and that specific rates/thresholds must always be sourced from current official guidance — without stating a specific percentage or rupee threshold as current fact.

## ⚠️ Critical Constraint

**This article deliberately does not state specific tax rates, exemption thresholds, or holding-period cutoffs.** Those are set by the Finance Act (as amended through each year's Union Budget) and change periodically. Any answer that needs a specific current number must point the user to the Income Tax Department's current official guidance (`incometax.gov.in`) or a qualified tax professional — never state a percentage or rupee figure from memory as if it were current law.

## Core Concepts

- A **capital gain** arises when a capital asset (e.g. equity shares, mutual fund units, property, bonds) is sold for more than its purchase cost (adjusted for certain allowable costs). A capital loss arises if sold for less.
- Indian tax law classifies capital gains as **short-term** or **long-term** based on how long the asset was held before sale — the specific holding-period cutoff that separates the two **differs by asset type** (e.g. listed equity shares/equity mutual funds have historically used a different cutoff than unlisted shares, debt instruments, or real estate) and can change — always confirm the current cutoff for the specific asset type in question.
- Short-term and long-term gains are generally taxed differently (different rates and/or different computation methods), and the exact treatment again depends on asset type and current law.
- Some instruments or circumstances have historically had specific exemptions, indexation benefits (adjusting purchase cost for inflation, where applicable), or threshold-based exemptions — these details are asset-type-specific and change-prone, so they must be verified against current official guidance rather than assumed.
- Capital gains are distinct from **dividend income**, which is taxed under its own rules — see "Dividend Income and Taxation."

## Misconceptions

- "Capital gains tax" is not a single flat rate in India — treatment varies by asset type and by short-term vs. long-term classification, both of which are rule-sets that change over time.
- A holding-period cutoff that applies to one asset type (e.g. listed equity) does not necessarily apply to another (e.g. property, unlisted shares, debt funds) — each has its own classification rule.
- Indexation (inflation-adjusting the purchase cost) has historically applied to some asset types/holding categories and not others, and its availability has changed over time — don't assume it applies universally.

# Risk Tolerance vs. Risk Capacity

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to separate **willingness** to take risk from **ability** to take risk, explain why both matter when thinking about an investment mix, and avoid telling any user how much risk they "should" take.

## Core Concepts

- **Risk tolerance** (the emotional side) is how comfortable someone is with the possibility of losses and price swings. SEC Investor.gov describes it as "your ability and willingness to lose some or all of your original investment in exchange for potentially greater returns".
- **Risk capacity** (the financial side) is how much loss a person's finances can absorb without derailing essential goals: income stability, emergency savings, debts and dependants, and how soon the money is needed.
- **Time horizon**: "the number of months, years, or decades you plan to invest to achieve your financial goal". Longer horizons may let investors "feel comfortable taking on riskier or more volatile investments", while short horizons may favour less volatile ones (SEC Investor.gov).
- The two can **disagree**. Someone with a steady income and no debts (high capacity) may still be anxious about any loss (low tolerance). Someone excited by risk (high tolerance) but supporting a family on one uncertain income (low capacity) is exposed to losses their finances cannot easily bear.
- SEC Investor.gov frames the practical test as depending on "when you will need the money, your goals, and whether you will be able to sleep at night if you purchase a risky investment".
- A questionnaire result is a **starting point for a conversation**, not a verdict. Tolerance can change after a market fall: people often feel more risk-averse once they have seen real losses.

## Worked Example

Two hypothetical investors, both 35, each with ₹5,00,000 to invest for retirement 25 years away. Illustration, not advice:

| | Asha | Ravi |
|---|---|---|
| Income | Stable, well above expenses | Variable, one earner, two dependants |
| Emergency savings | 12 months of expenses | 1 month of expenses |
| How a 30% fall in value feels | "Uncomfortable but I can wait" | "I'd be fine with it" |
| Capacity | High | Low |
| Tolerance | Moderate | High |

Ravi's stated comfort with risk is high, but his finances leave little room for a loss. A sensible discussion for him would look at both columns rather than only the one he feels confident about.

## Misconceptions

- "Risk tolerance is a fixed personality trait." It can shift with experience, age and circumstances.
- "If you can stomach it, you can afford it." Comfort and affordability are different questions.
- "A longer horizon removes risk." It gives more time to recover from falls, but does not remove the possibility of loss.

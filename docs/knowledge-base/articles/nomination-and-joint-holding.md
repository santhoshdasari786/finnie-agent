# Nomination and Joint Holding for Demat Accounts and Mutual Fund Folios

## Purpose

After reading this, the agent should be able to explain what nomination is, how it differs from joint holding and from a will, who may be a nominee, and what the nominee receives, while telling the user that the exact rules change and must be checked with the depository, fund house or SEBI.

## Core Concepts

- **Nomination** is naming one or more people who can receive the investments in a demat account or mutual fund folio if the holder dies. It is a record kept with the depository participant or fund house.
- **Joint holding** is different: two or more people are holders of the account or folio during their lifetimes, and the account is operated according to the mode chosen at opening. A joint holder is a holder now; a nominee is someone who may receive the holdings later.
- **Who can nominate:** the AMFI nomination page says only individuals holding units on their own behalf, singly or jointly, can nominate. A holder of a power of attorney, or a guardian investing for a minor, cannot nominate.
- **Who can be a nominee:** any person, including a minor (with a guardian named), and under AMFI's page also an NRI subject to exchange control rules. A company, partnership firm, HUF, society, or a trust other than a religious or charitable trust cannot be a nominee.
- **Number of nominees and shares:** AMFI's page says a person may nominate up to three people and state each one's share in whole numbers. Earlier SEBI rules reported in a search excerpt allowed more; the number has changed over time, so verify the current limit.
- **A nomination can be changed** at any time, any number of times, according to the AMFI page.
- **Nominee is not automatically the owner.** AMFI's page states that a nomination does not create an interest in the property after the holder's death and that a nominee may not necessarily acquire any title or beneficial interest. A SEBI FAQ excerpt (not fully read) says the nominee receives the assets as a trustee on behalf of the legal heirs. Who is legally entitled is decided by succession law and any will, not by nomination alone.
- **Recent change:** a SEBI circular dated 29 May 2026 modified the nomination norms for demat accounts and mutual fund folios. As read in a fund house's copy, for single-holder accounts and folios opened on or after the implementation date (stated as 1 September 2026) the investor must either provide a nomination or submit an opt-out declaration; for joint accounts nomination is optional; and all joint holders must agree to add or change a nominee. After the holder's death, nominees may continue in the same account or open separate ones.

Because these details are dated, always verify the current requirement with SEBI, the depository participant or the fund house before acting on any of it.

## Misconceptions

- "A nominee automatically owns the investments." Nomination names who can receive and claim them; legal ownership follows succession law and any will, and the nominee may hold them for the legal heirs.
- "A joint holder is the same as a nominee." A joint holder is an account holder during life; a nominee is only named for after death.
- "Nomination replaces a will." It does not, and the two can point to different people, which can cause disputes.
- "I can set it once and forget it." Nomination can be updated, and it is worth rechecking after life events such as marriage or the birth of a child; the rules themselves also change.
